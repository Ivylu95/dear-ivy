# Privacy Policy

**Effective:** 2026-09-27
**Last updated:** 2026-11-01
**Legal review:** Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. Written from published guidance, listed at the end; not legal advice.

This policy explains what Dear keeps about you, why, where it goes, who else
handles it, and how you see, move or remove it. It sits alongside the
[Safety Policy](SAFETY_POLICY.md) and the [Terms of Use](TERMS_OF_USE.md).

---

## In short

- **Your record is yours.** Dear writes what you tell it into a private record
  kept in your own accounts. The people who make Dear do not receive it, read it,
  sell it or use it to train anything.
- **Two services hold it.** Your conversations go through Anthropic, which
  runs Claude, the AI you talk to. Your record is stored with GitHub. Both are
  based in the United States and follow their own privacy policies. If you
  ask for reminders or check-ins on your phone, Telegram carries those messages.
- **It is sensitive, and treated that way.** Your record can hold your health,
  your feelings and the people in your life. It stays private to you, and the
  system checks that nothing about you leaks into any shared part of Dear.
- **Nothing is quietly deleted.** When something is corrected or tidied, the
  earlier version is kept in an archive, so your record never changes behind
  your back. You can still remove all of it whenever you choose.
- **You can see all of it, take all of it, and end all of it.** Everything about
  you is in one place, so each of those is one step.

---

## Who this policy is about

**You** are the person Dear keeps a record for. **The maker** is whoever writes
and publishes the Dear software. **The operator** is whoever set up your copy:
often you, sometimes someone who set it up for you. Where this policy says
"we", it means the maker and the operator together.

Dear is software that runs on accounts you hold. It is not a hosted service:
there is no Dear server that your words pass through, and no company database
they sit in.

---

## What is kept

**What you say.** Your conversations with Claude, the AI you talk to.

**What Dear writes down for you.** As you talk, Dear keeps a record so you never
have to explain yourself twice. It can include:

- events in your life, with their dates, and a journal of your days;
- how you are feeling, and what you have said about your mental and physical
  health, your therapy, and any medication you mention;
- your safety plan: warning signs, what helps, and who you would call;
- what you want, what helps you, and how you like to be spoken to;
- the people in your life, as you describe them.

**About other people.** When you talk about someone, Dear keeps what you said
about them, so you never have to introduce them again. They have not agreed to
this, so the record stays private to you and is never shared with them or anyone
else.

**Technical details.** Each change to your record is saved with the time it was
made. If you use the dashboard, a sign-in cookie keeps you signed in on that
device.

**What is not collected.** No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no
location, and no contacts from your phone.

---

## Why it is kept

Only so Dear can do what you use it for:

- to remember what you have told it, so you are never asked to start over;
- to keep your safety plan to hand, so the right people and numbers are there on
  a hard day;
- to let you read your own record back, through the dashboard or the files
  themselves.

It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never used by the maker to
train an AI model. By using Dear you agree to it being kept for these purposes,
and you can withdraw that agreement at any time by stopping and removing your
record (see Your rights, below).

---

## Where it is kept, and who can see it

- **Your record** is kept in a private repository on your own GitHub account.
  Only people who are members of that repository can open it. It is meant to be
  you alone.
- **If someone set Dear up for you**, they may have had access while doing so.
  Ask them to remove themselves once it is set up, and check your repository's
  member list: it should show only you.
- **Copies** exist on any computer you download your record to, or run the
  dashboard on.
- **Your conversations** are held by Anthropic in your Claude account, under
  Anthropic's terms. They are not part of your record, and deleting your record
  does not delete them.

**When Dear looks something up online**, the search never carries your name or
any detail of your life.

Nobody else receives your record. It is never shared with a therapist, a family
member, an employer or any authority by Dear. If you want someone to see part of
it, you choose what to show them.

## How it is protected

- Your repository is private, and should have only one member: you.
- The dashboard asks you to sign in, and refuses everyone if it is not set up
  correctly.
- Every time Dear writes to its own shared files, a check refuses anything that
  names you or the people in your life. A second check runs at the end of every
  turn.
- GitHub encrypts what it stores, but your record is not end-to-end encrypted:
  GitHub can technically read it, under its own policy.
- **What you can do:** turn on two-factor sign-in for your GitHub and Claude
  accounts, and keep the devices you use them on locked.

No system is perfectly secure. If the operator learns that someone has reached
your record who should not have, they will tell you as soon as they can, with
what happened and what you can do.

---

## How long it is kept

For as long as you keep using Dear, and until you remove it. Dear itself never
deletes anything from your record: a correction is added, and the earlier
version moves to an archive inside your record. This is so your record can
always be trusted to say what was said.

Removing your record entirely is always yours to do (below). Anthropic keeps your
conversations for as long as its own policy says.

## Your rights and choices

- **See everything.** Your whole record is one folder. Ask Dear what it holds
  about anything, or open the files or the dashboard.
- **Correct it.** Tell Dear what is wrong. It records the correction, and keeps
  the earlier version in the archive so the change is visible.
- **Take it with you.** Download the folder. It is plain text, readable anywhere,
  and moves to a new copy of Dear by being copied in.
- **Remove it.** Delete the repository on GitHub, and any copies you downloaded.
  Deleting the files alone is not enough, because the repository's history keeps
  earlier versions. Delete your Claude conversations separately, in Claude.
- **Stop at any time.** Nothing continues once you stop talking to it, except
  the reminders and check-ins you asked for, which keep arriving on their
  schedule until you ask Dear to stop them.
- **Complain.** If you are in Singapore and are unhappy with how your information
  was handled, you can contact the Personal Data Protection Commission at
  [pdpc.gov.sg](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg). If you live in the European Union or
  the United Kingdom, you can contact your data protection authority.

## For developers: how this is kept

Everything below is the developer's record: the rules each promise above answers to, how it is enforced, and where it stops. It was this policy's whole text until 2026-09-27, and is kept unchanged. The rules the policy itself answers to are in [`dev/specs/legal/policies.md`](../dev/specs/legal/policies.md).

**Everything personal about the person this is for lives in `data/`, and nowhere
else.** That one folder is the whole record: to see everything that is kept, to
back it up, to move it, or to remove it, it is the only thing anyone needs.

This is one of the core functions of the system, not a setting. A space someone
thinks out loud in is only safe to use if they can know, without trusting
anyone's judgement, exactly where every word of theirs went.

This page says what that promise means, how it is enforced, and where it stops.
The binding rules are in the specs, cited below; this page explains them and
points there, and never overrides them.

---

## Why one folder

**Privacy.** Whether a line is personal is a judgement, and a wrong judgement
looks exactly like a right one. Where the line sits answers it without any
judgement: inside `data/` it is personal, outside it nothing is. A check can
then prove the outside is clean, instead of anyone hoping it is.

**Portability.** The machinery around the record will be rewritten, replaced or
handed to someone else. The person must never be asked to re-explain themselves
when that happens. Because everything of theirs is in one folder, moving to new
machinery is a copy, not a project: copy `data/` into it, and it knows them.

**Control.** Backing up, locking down, exporting and removing are each one
operation against one folder. Spread across the tree, each would be a search,
and a search that misses a file still reports success.

---

## What this means in practice

**Personal** is anything about the person or the people in their life: names,
their words, events, dates from their life, health, what helps, what hurts. All
of it is written to `data/`, by the agent, as it comes up. The person never
files anything.

**Outside `data/`** everything is written for any reader:

- `.claude/`, `modules/`, `dev/specs/`, `policies/`, `ux/`, `README.md`: the
  machinery, its rules, its evidence base and its dashboard. Nothing personal,
  ever. The evidence base is searched and filed without the person's details
  in any query.
- `data-sample/`: an invented record, so the dashboard has something to show.
  Nothing in it is real.
- `data-sandbox/`: a blank record for testing the machinery. It is for invented
  content only, never real details, and can be reset at any time. It is not the
  record and does not move with it.
- `temp/`: scratch. Untracked, never saved, emptied at any time.
- Commit messages name the file touched, never what was written in it.

**Nothing in `data/` is deleted by the system.** Superseded content moves to
`data/archive/`, with a note of where it came from, so the record never quietly
changes.

**To move the record:** copy `data/` into a fresh copy of the machinery. On its
first session it reads what is there, migrates any older file shape, and keeps
the old copy in `data/archive/`.

## How it is enforced

Every one of these runs without anyone looking. None depends on the dashboard.

| When | What | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before any edit outside `data/` | The write is refused if it carries the person's name, a name from `data/core/people/`, or a line copied from the record | `.claude/hooks/privacy/guard-harness-privacy.mjs` |
| At the end of every turn | The whole tree outside `data/` is checked for the same, including what a shell command wrote past the guard; a leak stops the turn until it is moved | `.claude/hooks/privacy/check-privacy-at-stop.mjs` |
| On every `npm run check` and build | The same check, over everything | `ux/scripts/check-privacy.mjs` |
| Every session | Lessons about the machinery and anything about the person are filed in separate homes, and the second only ever in `data/` | `CLAUDE.md`, the skills |
| On every save | Only `data/` changes are committed as the record, with messages that name files only | `.claude/hooks/record/save-record.mjs` |

The rules these carry out: **ARC-004**, *"Everything personal lives in one place,
nowhere else, and comes away whole."*; **PRD-008**, *"The person's record lasts
as long as they want it, surviving the loss of any one place it is kept and any
change of the machinery, whole, with nothing asked of them again."*; **PRD-007**,
*"The person's record belongs to them alone — no one else can open it, the
developer included."*; **SFG-004**, *"A write that would put anything personal
outside the record is refused before it lands."*; **DAT-003**, *"Nothing in the
record is lost or changed without a trace."*; **PRV-001**, *"Only she can open
the record."* — in [`dev/specs/system/ARCHITECTURE.md`](../dev/specs/system/ARCHITECTURE.md),
[`dev/specs/PRODUCT.md`](../dev/specs/PRODUCT.md),
[`dev/specs/system/safeguards.md`](../dev/specs/system/safeguards.md),
[`dev/specs/data/record.md`](../dev/specs/data/record.md) and

## Where the promise stops

Said plainly, because a privacy promise that hides its limits is not one.

- **The checks catch names and copied lines, not paraphrases.** "The reader
  dislikes numbered lists" names nobody and is still about the person. That
  boundary rests on the agent's judgement when it writes, backed by the rules
  it reads first.
- **Git keeps history.** Removing `data/` removes the record from the current
  files, not from earlier commits. Erasing it completely means deleting the
  repository, or rewriting its history, and every copy of it.
- **The repository lives on GitHub**, privately, and on any machine it is cloned
  to or the dashboard is run from. Privacy comes from who can open it
  (**PRV-001**), not from keeping files out of it.
- **Conversations happen in Claude.** What the person says in a chat is processed
  and kept by the chat service under its own terms. The record in `data/` is
  what this system keeps; the chat history is not in it and is not governed by
  it.
- **The repository's own name** was chosen at setup and can carry a name. It sits
  outside `data/`, and only the owner can change it, on GitHub.
- **A test chat is only as clean as what is typed into it.** Real details typed
  while testing land in `data-sandbox/`, outside the record. Use invented ones.
- **A check that crashes stays quiet** rather than blocking every turn. The hook
  self-test, `node .claude/hooks/selftest.mjs`, is what proves each one is still
  alive.
