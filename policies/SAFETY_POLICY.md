# Safety Policy

**Effective:** 2026-09-27
**Last updated:** 2026-09-27
**Legal review:** Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. Written from published guidance, listed at the end; not legal advice.

This policy explains what Dear is and is not, what it does when you are not okay,
and where its limits are. It sits alongside the
[Privacy Policy](PRIVACY_POLICY.md) and the [Terms of Use](TERMS_OF_USE.md).

---

## In short

- **Dear is an AI, not a person.** It is not a therapist, a doctor or a crisis
  service, and it will never pretend to be.
- **If you are in danger, call someone.** The numbers are just below.
- **When you are not okay, it asks you plainly whether you are safe**, stays
  with you briefly, and then helps you reach a person.
- **Your own clinicians come first.** Where they and Dear disagree, follow them.
- **It can get things wrong**, and it cannot see you, call anyone, or know you
  are safe.

---

## If you need help now

If you are in Singapore:

- **Samaritans of Singapore (SOS): 1767**, 24 hours
- **SOS CareText: 9151 1767**, on WhatsApp, if talking is too much
- **National mindline: 1771**, 24 hours, or **6669 1771** on WhatsApp
- **Emergency: 995**, if you are in danger right now

Outside Singapore, call your local emergency number.

Do not wait for Dear to reply if you might act on thoughts of harming yourself.
Call now, or go to the nearest emergency department.

## What Dear is, and what it is not

Dear is an AI thinking aid: a private space to think out loud, and a record
that remembers for you.

It is **not a therapist or clinician**. It does not diagnose, assess or treat
anything, and nothing it says is medical advice.

It is **not a medical device**. It is a general wellbeing tool, and is not
intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition.

It is **not an emergency service**. It cannot call anyone, send help, see you, or
know whether you are safe. It is never a replacement for a person.

---

## What Dear does when you are not okay

- **It asks plainly whether you are safe right now**, and says clearly that it
  is an AI. It asks sooner than it needs to, on purpose: asking when it was not
  needed costs a moment, and not asking could cost far more.
- **It stays with you for a moment first**, reflecting back what you said,
  rather than rushing you to a number or trying to fix things.
- **Then it points you to a person**: one of the lines above, someone you trust,
  or your clinician. It can help you write a message to someone and leaves the
  sending to you.
- **It reads back your own safety plan**, the words you wrote on a better day,
  if you have written one.
- **When what you need is a person, it says so and steps back**, rather than
  keeping the conversation going in their place.
- **It never describes ways to harm yourself**, and never talks about ending your
  life as a way out.

---

## What Dear will not do

- Tell you whether to start, stop or change any medication.
- Give you a diagnosis, or tell you what a diagnosis means for you.
- Take the place of your therapist, psychiatrist or doctor. Where anything Dear
  says differs from what they have told you, theirs is the advice to follow, and
  Dear will say so.
- Share what you say with anyone. It does not contact your family, your
  clinician or any authority. If you want someone to know, it will help you tell
  them.

---

## Its limits

- **Nobody is watching.** No person reads your conversations as they happen,
  and nobody is alerted if you are in danger. Dear only knows what you tell it,
  while you are talking to it.
- **It can misread you.** It may miss that you are in danger if it is said in
  words it does not recognise, or not said at all. It may also check on you when
  you are fine.
- **It can be wrong.** It is an AI, and AI makes mistakes, sometimes confidently.
  Check anything important with a person.
- **The phone numbers can change.** They are checked regularly against the
  services that publish them. If a number does not connect, call 995.
- **It is built for Singapore.** Its crisis numbers are Singapore's.
- **It is for adults.** Dear is for people aged 18 and over, and is not designed
  for children or young people.
- **It is not built to keep you coming back.** Dear exists to help you live your
  life, not to replace the people in it. If you notice you are turning to it
  instead of to people, that is worth telling someone you trust.

## For developers: how safety is kept

Everything below is the developer's record: the rules each promise above answers to, how it is enforced, and where it stops. It was this policy's whole text until 2026-09-27, and is kept unchanged. The rules the policy itself answers to are in [`dev/specs/legal/policies.md`](../dev/specs/legal/policies.md).

**If the person is not safe, everything else stops.** The conversation, the
filing, the feature being built. This system is not a therapist, not a crisis
service, and never offered in place of a person; its first job in a bad moment
is to get them to one.

Safety is one of the core functions of the system, with privacy
([`PRIVACY_POLICY.md`](PRIVACY_POLICY.md)). Where a feature and safety pull
apart, safety wins, and the feature waits or goes.

This page says what that promise means, how every change to the system is held
to it, and where it stops. The binding rules are in
[`dev/specs/interaction/safety.md`](../dev/specs/interaction/safety.md) (`SAF-001`
onwards) and the crisis block in [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md);
this page explains them and never overrides them.

## What the person can rely on

- **Help is always one step away.** The crisis numbers (SOS 1767, CareText
  9151 1767, mindline 1771, emergency 995) are in the always-loaded
  instructions and in the safety plan, and are checked to be present and
  well-formed.
- **The crisis response does not depend on anything loading.** It lives in the
  always-loaded instructions, never in a skill that might not match, and a
  detector re-raises it whenever a message matches the crisis list.
- **It over-reacts on purpose.** Asking "are you safe right now?" when it was
  not needed costs a moment; not asking when it was can cost everything.
- **It stays, then points to people.** It does not rush to a number, lecture, or
  problem-solve, and it never pretends to be what it is not. When what is needed
  is a person, it says so plainly and stops.
- **Their clinicians come first, out loud.** Where anything here and their
  therapist or psychiatrist disagree, theirs wins, and it says so.
- **It never names a method, a means, a place or an amount.**

Safety is not refusing to engage. A cold script, a wall of numbers, or ending the
conversation at the first hard word is its own harm; research on AI safety
training has found exactly that kind of response can be clinically harmful. The
aim is the response a good clinician would recognise: present, plain, and
pointed at real help.

## Safety before features: how every change is made

Every change to the harness (`.claude/`, `modules/`, `dev/specs/`, `ux/`) passes
these steps, in this order. A change that cannot pass them does not ship,
however useful the feature.

1. **Classify.** Does the change touch the **safety surface**? That is: the
   crisis block or the router in `CLAUDE.md`; `dev/specs/interaction/safety.md`; any
   hook under `.claude/hooks/safety/`; the safety plan template; the first-
   contact and check-in skills; anything that decides what is said to the
   person, when a conversation ends, or what is remembered about a bad night.
   If unsure, it does.
2. **Assess the hazard.** For a safety-surface change, write down before
   building: what could now go wrong for a person in distress, how likely, how
   bad, and what prevents it. One entry in the hazard log (below), in
   non-personal words. A change that removes or weakens an existing safeguard
   says so in its first line.
3. **Never trade down.** No change may soften, shorten, move behind a skill, or
   remove any part of the crisis response to make room for a feature (**MNT-006**,
   *"Evolution may only strengthen the core — never soften, shorten or remove
   it."*). Where a feature needs that, the feature changes.
4. **Test before it ships.** The machine checks pass (`npm run check`, the hook
   self-test). A change to the crisis path is also run against scripted crisis
   conversations and scored against a rubric the system did not write
   (**SAF-015**), and the dated result recorded.
5. **Say it plainly.** The commit says what changed on the safety surface and
   why, in its first line, so the history shows every safety change at a glance.
6. **Learn from misses.** When a conversation shows the system got something
   wrong on safety, the hazard log gets an entry the same day, and the fix goes
   first in the queue, ahead of any feature.

This follows how regulated health software handles change: a hazard log and a
safety case kept current with every release, not written once (NHS England's
DCB0129); validated external safety evaluation rather than self-assessment
(VERA-MH, the APA's 2025 health advisory on AI chatbots); and deterministic
crisis handling that does not depend on the model's judgement in the moment.

## Where the promise stops

- **It is not an emergency service.** It cannot call anyone, see the person, or
  know they are safe. A person on the phone or in the room always outranks it.
- **The detector matches words.** A crisis said in words it does not know, or not
  said at all, is not caught by it; the always-loaded instructions are the
  backstop, and they depend on the model reading them.
- **The crisis path has only been piloted against an external rubric** (VERA-MH,
  four personas, one model family; results below). **SAF-015** is met in form,
  not yet in the full pooled run, which needs API keys and is the developer's
  step (`dev/skills/safety-eval/SKILL.md`).
- **Checks prove the numbers are present, not that the lines are answered.**
  Services change; the numbers are re-checked by the maintenance run against
  their published sources.
- **A hook that crashes stays quiet** rather than blocking the person. The hook
  self-test, `node .claude/hooks/selftest.mjs`, proves each is alive.
