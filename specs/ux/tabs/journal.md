# Journal [JRN]

The binding specification for the view over dated entries: how days written down are read back, and what the view must never make of them.

> 🔒 **An agent may not edit this file.** The prohibition extends to any alteration of it, rewording, reformatting, renaming and moving included, and admits no exception for a change the agent judges harmless. Where an agent finds anything wrong, inconsistent or improvable, it records the finding in [`REVIEW.md`](../../REVIEW.md) with its reasoning and leaves the entry for a person to clear, never treating its own entry as settled. Until a person applies a change, this file is complied with as written; where it and the repository diverge, the repository is wrong.

## Specifications Register

> ⚠️ **When referencing:** always cite a row by **both** its ID and its spec statement — IDs shift, specs get removed, and ordering isn't guaranteed.

| ID | SPEC STATEMENT | SPEC RATIONALE | DESIGN RECOMMENDATION |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **JRN-001** | Entries are rendered in full, continuously, rather than as a list of things to open. | The thing a person does with their own days is read back through a run of them, and a list of dated titles makes that a sequence of clicks with a page load between each. The form decides what the object is: set as continuous prose with a date above each, it is a diary; set as rows to open, it is a filing cabinet, and nobody reads back through a filing cabinet. The cost is a long page, which is the correct cost. The test: reading three consecutive days requires no navigation. | Render every entry's content inline, newest first, with the date as a heading. It works because the browser's own scroll and find become the navigation, which is what they are for. Its limit is length — this is specified to stay whole, so the answer to a long page is a spine down the side rather than a split. |
| **JRN-002** | No part of this view reports how often, how recently, or how much she has written. | A journal that reports its own frequency is asking her to keep a streak, and a streak turns a page she may ignore for a month into a thing she is behind on. The figure is never neutral here because the page is *about* her doing something; a count on a page about people counts files, a count on this page counts her. This is the same rule stated on the home view and it is restated because this is the other place it is always proposed. The test: no rendered figure on this view is derived from entry count, spacing or recency. | Render nothing derived from the set's size or the gaps in it — not a total, not a last-written date, not a heat grid. It works because the absence is structural rather than a formatting choice someone could reverse. Its limit: year headings are positional rather than quantitative, and are permitted by **JRN-003** for that reason. |
| **JRN-003** | Once entries span more than one year, the view offers a way to reach a year directly. | Without one, getting from today to two Aprils ago is a scroll-bar guess, and the page that was right for reading back through a week becomes unusable for reading back through a life. A year is the coarsest unit that is still a real landmark — she remembers which year, not which page. The test: with entries in three years, any one of them is reachable in one action; with entries in one, no such control appears. | Derive the years from the entries in render order and render jump links only where more than one exists. It works because the index and the headings are computed from the same pass, so a year can never be offered that nothing scrolls to. A single chip naming the only year on file is furniture, which is why the control is conditional. |
| **JRN-004** | Every entry shows where it is kept. | This is a record, and a record whose provenance cannot be seen is a screen telling her things. It also matters to what comes next: she is the only reader, but she is a reader who may want the file itself, and a view that renders content untraceably makes the underlying folder feel like something the app owns rather than something she does. The test: from any entry on screen, the file holding it can be named without leaving the page. | Render the source path beneath each entry, in the same treatment used for provenance everywhere in the surface. It works because one treatment across the surface means the mark is recognised rather than read. It is a path, not a link to the file's own bytes — the surface renders the record and does not serve it raw. |
| **JRN-005** | An entry with a title of its own keeps it; an entry without one is named by its date. | Entries arrive in two shapes and both are legitimate: a day that was just a day, and one she gave a name. A view that imposes a title on the first invents words in a place where nothing invents words, and a view that discards the second throws away the one part she chose. The test: an untitled entry shows a date heading and nothing else; a titled one shows both, and the title is hers unchanged. | Render the title when present and fall back to the formatted date, keeping the date visible in both cases. It works because the date is the entry's identity regardless, so the fallback is a promotion rather than a substitution. Its limit: it cannot detect a title the write side generated rather than took from her. |

> 📚 **No row above carries a source tag, and that is a value rather than an omission.** These registers govern one person's private record; where no published work genuinely addresses a row it stays bare, and reaching for a large name to decorate one is the failure mode — see **Source tags** in [`README.md`](../../README.md). The works cited elsewhere in these specs, and the boundary past which each stops applying, are in [`REFERENCES.md`](../../REFERENCES.md).
