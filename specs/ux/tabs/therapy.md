# Therapy [THR]

The binding specification for the view over appointments and the current clinical focus: what it shows, in what order, and the one thing it must say about itself.

> 🔒 **An agent may not edit this file.** The prohibition extends to any alteration of it, rewording, reformatting, renaming and moving included, and admits no exception for a change the agent judges harmless. Where an agent finds anything wrong, inconsistent or improvable, it records the finding in [`REVIEW.md`](../../REVIEW.md) with its reasoning and leaves the entry for a person to clear, never treating its own entry as settled. Until a person applies a change, this file is complied with as written; where it and the repository diverge, the repository is wrong.

## Specifications Register

> ⚠️ **When referencing:** always cite a row by **both** its ID and its spec statement — IDs shift, specs get removed, and ordering isn't guaranteed.

| ID | SPEC STATEMENT | SPEC RATIONALE | DESIGN RECOMMENDATION |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **THR-001** | The view states, unprompted and on every state of the page, that it is her own record and not a clinical one. | A page listing appointments under a heading that says *focus* looks like a file her clinician keeps, and looking like one is enough: she will weigh what it says against what she was actually told, and the wrong one may win. The sentence is not a disclaimer protecting the system; it is the thing that keeps this page in its place, and it costs a line. The test: the statement is present with no appointment on file and with fifty, and says which side wins in a disagreement. | Render it as a standing notice above the content, in plain words, naming the clinician as right where the two disagree. It works because the claim is made by the page rather than left to be inferred from the absence of clinical apparatus. Its limit: it cannot help with content the record itself has written in a clinical register, which is a write-side concern. |
| **THR-002** | What is due to be raised appears above what has already been discussed. | The person opening this page before an appointment wants the list she meant to bring, not the history of the last four. Ordering by recency is the obvious layout and it puts the one time-critical thing on the page below the least time-critical. The cost is small and repeated: an appointment where the thing that mattered went unsaid, again. The test: with both present, the outstanding items are reachable without scrolling past an appointment write-up. | Lift the outstanding items of the appointment group above the appointment history, marked as being in her words. It works because the group is identified in the record rather than by position, and the marking stops the lift reading as the surface's own summary of what she should say. Its limit: only outstanding items are lifted; a settled one belongs to that list's own history. |
| **THR-003** | Anything kept for her to say is shown verbatim. | It is kept verbatim precisely so it can be carried into a room, and a tidied version is a different sentence — usually a calmer one, which is the opposite of useful. The words she could not get out are the record's most fragile content and the surface is the last place they should be improved. The test: the rendered text matches the record character for character, with no truncation and no ellipsis. | Render the item's text as stored, with no clamping, summarising or reflow that drops content. It works because there is nothing between the file and the page to lose anything. Its limit: it says nothing about how long an item may be, which is a write-side question. |
| **THR-004** | The current focus and the appointments it came from are shown as distinct things. | A focus is a short standing statement; an appointment is a dated account. Collapsed together, the focus reads as a summary of the most recent appointment — which it is not, and which would make it change every time she goes rather than when the work changes. The cost is a page that quietly misrepresents how the work is going. The test: the focus is identifiable without reading an appointment, and carries its own provenance. | Render the focus in its own section above the history, from its own source, with its own path shown. It works because two sources rendered with two provenances cannot be read as one thing. Its limit: nothing here verifies that the focus is current, which is an upkeep concern. |
| **THR-005** | Each state of emptiness says what it is and what fills it. | Three different pages are all "empty": no focus written, no appointments written up, and nothing here at all. Rendering one message for all three tells a reader nothing, and rendering silence tells them something is broken. This page is also the one where an empty section is most likely to be read as a failure on her part rather than as a record that has not reached that yet. The test: each empty section names what belongs in it, without instruction and without implying she should be filing it. | State per section what goes there and where it comes from, phrased as description rather than as a prompt. It works because the phrasing is the whole difference between a record and a form. Its limit: this row governs wording, not whether a section appears — that is **NOW-004**'s rule, applied here. |

> 📚 **No row above carries a source tag, and that is a value rather than an omission.** These registers govern one person's private record; where no published work genuinely addresses a row it stays bare, and reaching for a large name to decorate one is the failure mode — see **Source tags** in [`README.md`](../../README.md). The works cited elsewhere in these specs, and the boundary past which each stops applying, are in [`REFERENCES.md`](../../REFERENCES.md).
