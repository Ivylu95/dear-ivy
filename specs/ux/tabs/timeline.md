# Timeline [TML]

The binding specification for the view over the spine of the record: every dated event, in order, and what the surface may and may not do to that order.

> 🔒 **An agent may not edit this file.** The prohibition extends to any alteration of it, rewording, reformatting, renaming and moving included, and admits no exception for a change the agent judges harmless. Where an agent finds anything wrong, inconsistent or improvable, it records the finding in [`REVIEW.md`](../../REVIEW.md) with its reasoning and leaves the entry for a person to clear, never treating its own entry as settled. Until a person applies a change, this file is complied with as written; where it and the repository diverge, the repository is wrong.

## Specifications Register

> ⚠️ **When referencing:** always cite a row by **both** its ID and its spec statement — IDs shift, specs get removed, and ordering isn't guaranteed.

| ID | SPEC STATEMENT | SPEC RATIONALE | DESIGN RECOMMENDATION |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **TML-001** | The view presents the spine in two tiers: the shape of each period, then the events themselves. | A ledger read front to back is a ledger nobody finishes. The orienting tier exists so a reader can find the range that matters in one read rather than scrolling for it, and it is the same mechanism a session uses to avoid sweeping the folder. Drop it and the page still renders every line, which is why the loss is invisible: the cost is paid by the reader who cannot tell a bad quarter from a quiet one without reading forty rows. The test: the shape of a period is legible without reading any event in it. | Render the orienting section above the entries, unfiltered and always, and let the entries below carry the filtering. It works because the two tiers answer different questions and a control that narrows one has no business narrowing the other. Its limit: the orienting tier is written during upkeep and can lag the entries, so it is never the thing a reader is told to trust for detail. |
| **TML-002** | The view may reverse the ledger's order and may change nothing else about it. | The file is appended to, so oldest-first is right for it; a reader arriving at a page wants the most recent thing at the top. Both are correct for their own medium and the flip is the one presentational liberty this view has. Every other rearrangement — grouping, merging, promoting, hiding — is the surface deciding what her history was, which is not its decision to make. The test: with the flip undone, the rendered sequence is the file's sequence exactly. | Reverse once at the read and treat the result as immutable downstream. It works because a single reversal at a named boundary is auditable, where a sort applied at render is a comparator nobody re-reads. It does not license sorting by anything other than the date the event happened — a line's position is its date, never when it was told. |
| **TML-003** | An event tells the reader when it happened and, where they differ, when it was told. | A thing described three weeks late is filed under the day it happened, and a view showing only that date quietly asserts the record had it at the time. The gap is information: it is usually how long something took to become sayable. Collapsing the two dates into one loses that, and loses it in the direction that flatters the record. The test: an entry carrying both dates renders both, and an entry carrying one renders one without implying the other. | Carry the told date as a separate field and render it beside the event date only when present. It works because absence is then meaningful rather than defaulted, so nothing has to guess. Its limit: the record can only mark a delay it was told about, so an unmarked entry is not proof of a same-day account. |
| **TML-004** | Anything the record marked as a reading rather than her account stays marked in the view. | Her words are the record; a reading of them is not, and a page that renders both in one face has turned an inference into testimony. A future session reads this view's underlying file as fact, and so does she — the difference between "this is what happened" and "this is what something concluded about what happened" is the difference between a record and a verdict. The test: an entry marked as inferred in the file is visibly distinguished in the view, and the distinction survives every filter. | Strip the marker from the sentence and render it as a stated tag reading in plain words that it is not her account. It works because the marker then cannot be mistaken for part of the event text, and the plain wording does not require the reader to know a convention. Its limit is that it depends entirely on the write side having marked it; the view cannot detect an unmarked inference. |
| **TML-005** | Narrowing the view never changes, hides or renumbers the record beneath it. | A filter is a reading aid and reads as a state of the record — a page showing four entries looks like a record holding four entries. The risk is not confusion for its own sake: a person who cannot tell a narrowed view from an empty stretch of her life concludes something is missing. The test: any narrowing states what it is showing and out of how much, and clearing it is reachable from the narrowed state without knowing how it was reached. | Filter client-side over the full rendered set, with the active narrowing and the full total both stated on screen. It works because nothing has to be re-fetched to widen again, so the way back is never a failed request. Its limit: it presumes the whole set is rendered, which stops being reasonable at a size this file is already specified to split at. |
| **TML-006** | Every pointer a line carries resolves to somewhere in the surface, or is shown as plain text. | The whole mechanism of the spine is that one line points at where the detail lives; a pointer that does nothing when clicked breaks the promise the view makes in its own subtitle. But a dead link is worse than no link — it teaches the reader that pointers here are decorative. The test: every pointer either navigates or is visibly not a link, and no pointer navigates to a view that cannot show that file. | Resolve the path through one shared mapping and return nothing where no view holds it, rendering the raw path in that case. It works because the pointer is still information even where this surface has nowhere to send it, and because one mapping keeps the rail, the line and the destination from disagreeing. Its limit: the mapping is by path shape, so a file moved without the mapping being updated silently degrades to plain text rather than failing loudly. |

> 📚 **No row above carries a source tag, and that is a value rather than an omission.** These registers govern one person's private record; where no published work genuinely addresses a row it stays bare, and reaching for a large name to decorate one is the failure mode — see **Source tags** in [`README.md`](../../README.md). The works cited elsewhere in these specs, and the boundary past which each stops applying, are in [`REFERENCES.md`](../../REFERENCES.md).

## Unwritten

- Whether a correction and the line it supersedes are ever shown together, and which one carries the other's date.
- Whether the view may ever be entered at a date rather than at the top.
