### What this means for a *private, single-user, record-keeping* assistant

Most of this literature is written about consumer products with millions of users, an
engagement business model, and no named person behind them. Three of its conclusions
land differently here, and the difference is worth stating rather than assuming:

- **Memory.** APA's mitigation for dependency is to *limit* memory. This repo's fourth
  principle is that being remembered **is** the service. These are not reconcilable by
  splitting the difference. The distinction that holds: the harm APA describes is
  *anthropomorphic* memory — memory that manufactures the illusion of a relationship
  (warmth, flattery, a persona that missed her). The good Jewell et al. describe is
  *episodic and semantic* memory — a record, so she never re-explains who someone is.
  Be maximal on the second and deliberately thin on the first. Raised as a proposal
  2026-09-20.
- **Human oversight.** The single largest gap in the field — ongoing clinician oversight,
  present in 4 of the reviewed interventions — is structurally absent here. The nearest
  substitute is the rule that her clinician outranks this, plus the push toward a person
  in the room. That is instruction, not machinery, and should be counted as such.
- **Deletion.** Every framework in this run requires the user be able to delete or retire
  what is held about them. This repo forbids deletion outright. Archival answers
  *correction* and *retiring a stale pattern*; it does not answer *deletion*. Worth
  deciding on purpose rather than by inheritance.
